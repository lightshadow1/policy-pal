## Purpose & Philosophy

This company was built on the belief that exceptional engineering talent is not constrained by geography. Our remote-first culture has been a genuine competitive differentiator — enabling us to recruit from global talent markets, reduce real estate overhead, and build high-performing teams on the basis of outcomes rather than presence. This policy formalizes that foundation while introducing the structure, accountability, and legal safeguards necessary to scale it responsibly.

Flexibility and high performance are not in conflict. This policy is designed to maximize both by being **intentional, equitable, and defensible** — bold enough to differentiate us in competitive talent markets, and structured enough to give team leads and employees the clarity they need to succeed.

---

## Scope

This policy applies to all full-time employees on engineering teams, regardless of seniority level or location. It does not apply to independent contractors, whose arrangements are governed by separate agreements. Any conflict between this policy and local employment law will be resolved in favor of legal compliance for the relevant jurisdiction.

---

## Core Remote Work Framework

### Default Posture: Remote-First

Engineering team members are permitted to work remotely as their default arrangement. There is no blanket in-office attendance requirement. Team leads are empowered to establish reasonable, role-specific expectations within the guardrails defined in this policy, provided those expectations are applied consistently within their teams and documented in advance.

### In-Person Expectations

While remote is the default, in-person collaboration has recognized value — particularly for onboarding, mentorship, and complex cross-functional work. The following guidelines apply:

- **Senior and mid-level engineers:** Minimum quarterly in-person sessions (team offsites, planning sprints, or equivalent). Specific cadence to be set by team lead in coordination with HR.
- **Junior engineers and new hires (first 90 days):** Enhanced in-person or synchronous mentorship expectations apply. Team leads must document a structured onboarding plan that accounts for mentorship proximity needs, whether in-person or via scheduled remote pairing.
- **Company-wide or team events:** Attendance expectations will be communicated with no less than **30 days' notice**, with accommodation processes available (see Section: Accommodations).

### Performance Accountability

Flexibility is granted in exchange for clarity on outcomes. All remote-eligible employees must have:

1. **Documented performance objectives** reviewed at minimum quarterly, aligned to team and company goals.
2. **Regular check-ins** with their manager — cadence to be defined per team but no less than biweekly.
3. **Clear deliverable standards** so that output, not hours logged, serves as the primary measure of performance.

Team leads who observe performance concerns in remote arrangements are expected to address them through the standard performance management framework, not through ad hoc changes to individual remote work terms.

## Fairness, Consistency, and Equitable Application

This policy applies uniformly across all engineering team members at equivalent levels. Exceptions or modifications to standard arrangements must be:

- Documented in writing by the team lead
- Reviewed and approved by People & Culture
- Applied without regard to protected characteristics

**No informal exceptions** will be permitted. Undocumented individual arrangements — even well-intentioned ones — create inequity and legal exposure. Any pattern of exceptions that could constitute disparate impact will be reviewed by Legal and HR jointly.

Employees who believe this policy has been applied inconsistently or unfairly may raise concerns through the standard grievance process outlined in the Employee Handbook.

## Financial Framework

### Cost-Benefit Accountability

Remote work arrangements must be financially sustainable and measurable. Before full rollout or material expansion of this policy, Finance will produce a **12-month cost-benefit projection** covering:

- **Upfront costs:** Technology infrastructure, security tooling, and home office stipends
- **Realizable savings:** Office space reduction net of existing lease obligations
- **Retention value:** Modeled cost avoidance from reduced attrition, benchmarked against the estimated 150–200% of annual salary cost to replace a senior engineer

### Stipends and Equipment

Home office stipends and equipment budgets, where provided, will be:

- Defined with clear per-employee caps approved by Finance
- Tied to documented business need (e.g., secure remote access requirements)
- Reviewed annually as part of the budget cycle

### Productivity Baseline and Metrics

People & Culture and Finance will jointly establish a productivity baseline prior to or concurrent with this policy's effective date. Metrics will be reviewed at the 6-month and 12-month marks to assess whether output is maintained or improved under remote arrangements.

## Legal and Compliance Requirements

### Jurisdictional Compliance

Employees working outside their home state or country — whether on a permanent or extended basis — must notify People & Culture and Legal **prior to** beginning work in the new location. The company maintains a **multi-jurisdiction compliance checklist** (maintained by Legal, available upon request) covering:

- State and country-specific wage and hour laws
- Payroll tax registration obligations
- Benefits eligibility implications
- Work authorization requirements for international locations

Failure to notify in advance may result in the remote arrangement being suspended until compliance is confirmed.

### Data Security and IP Protection

All remote work arrangements are subject to the company's **Security Annex for Remote Access** (companion document, incorporated by reference). Key requirements include:

- Use of company-approved devices or IT-secured personal devices for all work involving proprietary code, data, or systems
- VPN or equivalent secure access protocols when accessing internal systems
- Prohibition on working from unsecured public networks without approved security tooling
- Prompt reporting of lost or compromised devices to IT Security

Engineers handling sensitive IP are subject to additional access controls as defined by the Security Annex.

### Classification and Contractor Boundaries

Remote work terms in this policy apply exclusively to employees. Managers must not extend remote work flexibility arrangements to contractors in ways that blur classification lines. Any uncertainty about worker classification should be escalated to Legal before arrangements are modified.

### Policy Approval and Defensibility

This policy is effective upon sign-off from the Founder, People & Culture, Finance, and Legal. The approval log will be maintained by People & Culture. A defined review cycle of **12 months** applies, with an interim check-in at 6 months. Any material changes to this policy require the same four-party sign-off before taking effect.

## Implementation and Rollout

### Notice and Transition

Any changes to existing work arrangements resulting from this policy will be communicated to affected employees with a minimum of **30 days' written notice**. Employees with established remote arrangements that predate this policy will not have those arrangements reduced without a separate discussion and adequate notice period.

### Accommodations

Employees with caregiving responsibilities, long-distance commutes, disabilities, or other circumstances requiring accommodation should contact People & Culture to initiate a review. Accommodation requests will be assessed individually, documented, and resolved within 10 business days of submission.

### 60-Day Post-Rollout Review

People & Culture will conduct a structured **60-day check-in** following this policy's rollout. The check-in will include:

- A pulse survey distributed to all engineering team members
- A structured debrief with team leads
- A written summary of findings and any recommended adjustments submitted to policy owners

Findings from this review may prompt interim revisions outside the standard 12-month cycle if material concerns are identified.
